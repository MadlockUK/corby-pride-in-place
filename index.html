Darker always means more deprived. The other 28 neighbourhoods in the Corby Town Council area are shown faded for comparison; switch to "Whole town" to rank all 37.

Boundaries: ONS Open Geography Portal — LSOA Dec 2021 BGC V5 and MSOA Dec 2021 BGC V3 (the same generalised boundaries used in the August 2026 evidence pack). Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2021; ONS, Open Government Licence v3.0.

Data: English Indices of Deprivation 2025 (MHCLG; ranks of 33,755, 1 = most deprived) · Census 2021 via Nomis (ONS) · Claimant count September 2026 (ONS/DWP via Nomis) · DfE school performance 2024/25. Compiled in the Pride in Place Deprivation Data Pack; Census figures for the two Exeter LSOAs added from Nomis in October 2026 using the same method. Claimant rates are the ONS published share of residents aged 16–64 for every LSOA; the whole-area rate is approximate.

Boundary: this map's Pride in Place boundary is the published MSOA E02005617 (Kingswood and Hazel Leys) plus the Exeter estate: LSOAs E01026951 (North Northamptonshire 005C) and E01026950 (005B, which takes in Burghley Drive and Brayford Avenue). The two Exeter LSOAs sit in MSOA E02005616, so they are outside the published MSOA; tick the option above to see it. 005B's IMD 2025 ranks come from the MHCLG Local Deprivation Explorer (October 2026). School locations are approximate.

Whole town: the 37 neighbourhoods (LSOAs) inside Corby civil parish, the Corby Town Council area (ONS output area to parish lookup). For the 28 outside the Pride in Place boundary, deprivation ranks come from the English Indices of Deprivation 2025 file and Census figures from Nomis, worked out the same way as the data pack (checked: the method reproduces the pack's figures for all nine Pride in Place neighbourhoods). Town council wards are the four parish wards set by the North Northamptonshire (Electoral Changes) Order 2024 (Corby West 5 councillors, Kingswood 4, Lloyds 3, Oakley 5), drawn as the May 2025 council wards inside the parish (ONS boundaries). Six neighbourhoods straddle a ward line and are listed under the ward that holds most of them.

Benefits: DWP Stat-Xplore: people on Universal Credit (May 2026) and Personal Independence Payment cases with entitlement (Jul 2026), by home neighbourhood, shown as a share of all residents (Census 2021). Recent months are provisional and can be revised. Health and caring: Census 2021 (TS037, TS039) via Nomis.

Crime: street-level crime and anti-social behaviour from data.police.uk (Home Office, Open Government Licence v3.0), Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, counted into each neighbourhood by location. Locations are anonymised to nearby map points, so counts near edges can fall either side; crimes with no location are left out. Town-centre neighbourhoods have high rates because of shops and the night-time economy, not because of the people who live there. Street faults: reports made to North Northamptonshire Council on its FixMyStreet site (public Open311 feed), Oct 2025 to Sep 2026, counted by location. These are residents' reports, not checked faults, and one fault can be reported more than once. The feed only carries highways reports (potholes, pavements, overgrown hedges, signs, drains and lights), not bins or fly-tipping. "Still open" is as of 2026-10-29.
