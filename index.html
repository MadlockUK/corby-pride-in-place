Kingswood, Hazel Leys & Exeter

All nine neighbourhoods in the Pride in Place area (each an LSOA, a small statistical area of roughly 1,500 people): Kingswood and Hazel Leys (007A–007G) and the Exeter estate (005B and 005C, including Burghley Drive). Darker always means more deprived. The other 28 neighbourhoods in the Corby Town Council area are shown faded for comparison; switch to "Whole town" to rank all 37.

Boundaries: ONS Open Geography Portal — LSOA Dec 2021 BGC V5 and MSOA Dec 2021 BGC V3 (the same generalised boundaries used in the August 2026 evidence pack). Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2021; ONS, Open Government Licence v3.0.

Data: English Indices of Deprivation 2025 (MHCLG; ranks of 33,755, 1 = most deprived) · Census 2021 via Nomis (ONS) · Claimant count September 2026 (ONS/DWP via Nomis) · DfE school data 2024/25 (see Schools below). Compiled in the Pride in Place Deprivation Data Pack; Census figures for the two Exeter LSOAs added from Nomis in October 2026 using the same method. Claimant rates are the ONS published share of residents aged 16–64 for every LSOA; the whole-area rate is approximate.

Boundary: this map's Pride in Place boundary is the published MSOA E02005617 (Kingswood and Hazel Leys) plus the Exeter estate: LSOAs E01026951 (North Northamptonshire 005C) and E01026950 (005B, which takes in Burghley Drive and Brayford Avenue). The two Exeter LSOAs sit in MSOA E02005616, so they are outside the published MSOA; tick the option above to see it. 005B's IMD 2025 ranks come from the MHCLG Local Deprivation Explorer (October 2026). School locations are approximate.

Whole town: the 37 neighbourhoods (LSOAs) inside Corby civil parish, the Corby Town Council area (ONS output area to parish lookup). For the 28 outside the Pride in Place boundary, deprivation ranks come from the English Indices of Deprivation 2025 file and Census figures from Nomis, worked out the same way as the data pack (checked: the method reproduces the pack's figures for all nine Pride in Place neighbourhoods). Town council wards are the four parish wards set by the North Northamptonshire (Electoral Changes) Order 2024 (Corby West 5 councillors, Kingswood 4, Lloyds 3, Oakley 5), drawn as the May 2025 council wards inside the parish (ONS boundaries). Six neighbourhoods straddle a ward line and are listed under the ward that holds most of them.

Benefits: DWP Stat-Xplore: people on Universal Credit (May 2026) and Personal Independence Payment cases with entitlement (Jul 2026), by home neighbourhood, shown as a share of all residents (Census 2021). Recent months are provisional and can be revised. Health and caring: Census 2021 (TS037, TS039) via Nomis.

Pensioners, carers and child poverty: DWP Stat-Xplore by home neighbourhood: Pension Credit claimants, Attendance Allowance and Carer's Allowance cases with entitlement (Mar 2026), and children aged under 16 in relative low-income families before housing costs (2024/25, the latest year; the figures for recent years are provisional). Shares use Census 2021 populations (aged 65 and over, 16 and over, or under 16), so they are a guide rather than an official rate. State Pension age is 66, so the 65+ base slightly understates the Pension Credit and Attendance Allowance shares. DWP adds small random adjustments to protect privacy, so counts for single neighbourhoods can be off by a few.

Crime: street-level crime and anti-social behaviour from data.police.uk (Home Office, Open Government Licence v3.0), Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, counted into each neighbourhood by location. Locations are anonymised to nearby map points, so counts near edges can fall either side; crimes with no location are left out. Town-centre neighbourhoods have high rates because of shops and the night-time economy, not because of the people who live there. Street faults: reports made to North Northamptonshire Council on its FixMyStreet site (public Open311 feed), Oct 2025 to Sep 2026, counted by location. These are residents' reports, not checked faults, and one fault can be reported more than once. The feed only carries highways reports (potholes, pavements, overgrown hedges, signs, drains and lights), not bins or fly-tipping. "Still open" is as of 2026-10-29.

Fuel poverty: Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ), Sub-regional fuel poverty data 2026, which covers 2024 (published May 2026; Open Government Licence v3.0). It uses the government's Low Income Low Energy Efficiency measure: a household is fuel poor if its home is rated band D or below for energy efficiency and, after paying for energy, its income is below the poverty line. Neighbourhood figures are modelled estimates; DESNZ says to use them to spot areas of high or low fuel poverty, not to track one neighbourhood over time. The Pride in Place and Corby town figures add up the neighbourhood estimates; North Northants and England are DESNZ's own figures from the same release.

Energy ratings (EPCs): the Energy Performance Certificate register (Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, "Get energy performance of buildings data"; Open Government Licence v3.0), certificates lodged 2008 to Oct 2026. For each property we use its latest certificate, and place it in a neighbourhood using its postcode. Rates are shares of homes that have a certificate, not of all homes. Only homes that have been sold, let or improved since 2008 (and all new builds) have one, so some homes are missing, and a rating can be many years old: about 37 in 100 of the ratings here are more than 10 years old. Coverage is uneven: in Corby rented homes, especially privately rented ones, are better covered than owner-occupied homes (and about 1 in 5 certificates does not record who lives there, so this is only a guide). Some neighbourhoods show more certificates than 2021 Census households because homes have been built since 2021. North Northants and England use the same method on every certificate. "Most common heating fuel" is the main fuel recorded on the certificate, which is an assessor's description rather than a meter reading.

Health: OHID (Office for Health Improvement and Disparities) Fingertips, Local Health profile (Open Government Licence v3.0). Life expectancy and healthy life expectancy at birth (2019 to 2023; healthy life expectancy is the years a person can expect to live in good health, from the 2021 Census), the share of Reception and Year 6 children measured as obese by the National Child Measurement Programme (three school years, 2022/23 to 2024/25) and emergency hospital admissions for all causes (2016/17 to 2020/21, England = 100, adjusted for age and sex). These are published for MSOAs (wider areas of several neighbourhoods, roughly 5,000 to 15,000 people), not for single neighbourhoods, so each neighbourhood shows the value for the MSOA it sits in. The 37 neighbourhoods sit in eight MSOAs: seven of the nine Pride in Place neighbourhoods are in Corby Kingswood and the two Exeter ones are in Corby Town. The Pride in Place and Corby town figures are averages of those MSOA values weighted by population, so they are approximate. Life expectancy for one MSOA (Snatchill) is hidden for men because there are too few deaths. Emergency admissions are the oldest figures here and include the Covid years. Obesity here is for children only; OHID publishes no small-area obesity figure for adults.

Buses: Bus Open Data Service (Department for Transport; Open Government Licence v3.0), the East Midlands timetable feed (version 22 Oct 2026). We count scheduled departures at each stop for Tuesday 3 November (a normal term-time weekday), Saturday 7 and Sunday 8 November 2026, and ignore stops where buses do not pick up. "Within 400 m" is straight-line distance (about a five-minute walk) from the postcode of each home address, counting each postcode by its number of homes with an energy certificate as a stand-in for homes; it ignores hills, busy roads and the real walking route. Timetabled does not mean it ran: buses are late, re-timed or cancelled. Demand-responsive and school-only services may be missing or counted. Pride in Place and Corby town figures are averages of the neighbourhoods weighted by homes; North Northants uses the same method on all its postcodes; England is not calculated because that would need every region's timetables.

Road collisions: Department for Transport road safety data (STATS19), the police-recorded collisions that caused injury, 2021 to 2025 (Open Government Licence v3.0). Minor collisions that are not reported to the police are missing, and how serious an injury is judged to be can differ between police forces, so be careful comparing severity. Each collision is placed in the neighbourhood it happened in, using its map reference. Rates are per 1,000 residents (Census 2021) added up over the five years, so they are a guide only; busy roads raise a neighbourhood's figure without its residents being the ones hurt. North Northants and England use the same counts and Census 2021 populations. Stop and search: data.police.uk stop and search records for Northamptonshire Police, Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, counted by location. Locations are rounded to nearby map points and stops with no location are left out. The counts are small, and the figures show where the police searched people, not where those people live. No North Northants or England comparison is shown, because the police force covers North and West Northamptonshire and England would need every force. The map shows the type of search, the outcome and the age band of each person only: ethnicity and other personal details are in the police data but are not shown here.

Food hygiene and takeaways: Food Standards Agency Food Hygiene Rating Scheme (Open Government Licence v3.0), North Northamptonshire Council's ratings as published on 30 Oct 2026. Ratings run from 0 (urgent improvement necessary) to 5 (very good) and show how well a business handled food hygiene, handling and cleanliness at its last inspection, which may be a year or more ago. Each business is placed in a neighbourhood by its map location (or its postcode where there is none); businesses on industrial estates and in villages outside the 37 neighbourhoods are not counted. Only businesses with a 0 to 5 rating are in the percentages, and neighbourhoods with fewer than five are left blank. Takeaways are the businesses the council registers as a "Takeaway/sandwich shop", so sandwich shops are included and restaurants, pubs, mobile vans and fast-food restaurants registered as restaurants are not. The per-1,000 rate uses Census 2021 residents. North Northants and England use the same Food Standards Agency data (England is the 298 English councils in the scheme).

House prices: HM Land Registry Price Paid Data (Open Government Licence v3.0): every home sale in North Northamptonshire postcodes completed between 1 November 2023 and 28 September 2026, the latest the data holds. Each sale is placed in a neighbourhood by its postcode and the middle (median) price is shown, rounded to the nearest £100. Neighbourhoods with fewer than ten sales are left blank. It covers all homes sold, including new builds, flats and leasehold, so the mix of homes sold shapes the figure. The Pride in Place and Corby town figures use all their sales together; North Northants uses the same method. England is not shown because the full England sales file is a very large download. Sale prices are not valuations, and homes that did not sell are not counted. Private rents: Office for National Statistics, Price Index of Private Rents, monthly statistics released 21 October 2026, average monthly rent for September 2026 across the whole North Northamptonshire council area and England. Official rent figures are not published for neighbourhoods, so every neighbourhood shows the same council-area figure. They cover new and existing tenancies and are official statistics still in development.

Schools: every open school in or serving Corby: the 23 inside the 37 neighbourhoods and three just outside the Town Council area (Corby Business Academy, Priors Hall and Little Stanion Primary), from the Department for Education's Get Information about Schools register (Gretton and the two Weldon schools are left out). Figures are for the 2024/25 school year from Department for Education statistics (Open Government Licence v3.0): pupil numbers, free school meals and first language from the school census; special educational needs from the SEN statistics; absence from the pupil absence statistics; and test and exam results from Key Stage 2 (end of primary) and Key Stage 4 (GCSE) results. Pride in Place schools are the four that were already on the map. Dots are at the postcode, so they are approximate. "Free school meals" is the share of pupils known to be eligible at the January 2025 census; England figures are for state-funded schools of the same type (primary, secondary or special) worked out from the same files. Test and exam results are not shown for nurseries, special schools or infant schools. Attainment depends on who a school admits as well as how well it teaches, and small schools have very changeable figures.
